Generic FSM vs. Telecom-Specific Tools for Telecom Contractors

By Gil Ramirez March 26, 2026 5 min read

Two Kinds of Field Software

Generic field service management (FSM) software is built around the service call: a ticket, a technician, a time slot, a visit. That fits many trades, and it fits them well.

Telecom construction is billed differently. Fiber crews are paid by the code and the quantity: feet of conduit, feet of cable, drops, splices. The record the invoice depends on is the as-built map, not the visit.

Where Generic FSM Fits

If your work is a series of short visits billed by the hour or at a flat rate, scheduling and dispatch are the problem, and generic FSM is built to solve it. Some contractors who run service work alongside construction use it for that side of the business.

Where It Gets Stretched on Telecom Work

On construction and drop work, the stretch shows up in the same places. Units-based billing gets forced into line items built for visits. The as-built map lives outside the system. Footage comes in on a sheet and gets typed in again. The office rebuilds the invoice from notes.

The software isn't wrong. It was built for a different kind of job.

Side by Side

Question Generic FSM (typical) Telecom-specific tools
What a job is A service visit A segment of work on an as-built map
How work is recorded Time, status, and notes on a ticket Rate codes and quantities marked on the map
Where quantities come from Typed in by the tech or the office Counted from the marks on the map
What the invoice is built from Hours, flat rates, or line items entered after the visit The submitted tally, priced from the rate card for that ISP or prime
What the office checks The ticket and its notes The marked map and the tally

What a Telecom-Specific Tool Has to Do

Let's be specific about what the system has to do for you to bill the work:

  • Mark the map. The crew places each code and quantity on the as-built, where the work was done.
  • Submit from the field. The crew submits the marked map once, from the site. Each submission is saved with who submitted it and when.
  • Tally from the marks. Footage and units add up by rate code from the marks, not from a count typed in later.
  • Invoice from the tally. The office drafts the invoice from the submitted tally, each line priced from the rate card for that ISP or prime. The invoice can go out the day the job is submitted.
  • One record per job. The office job list shows how many times each job's map has been submitted and the date of the last submission.

Questions to Ask Any Vendor

  • Can the crew record codes and quantities on the map itself?
  • Are quantities counted from what the crew marked, or typed in again?
  • Can each customer's rate card price the invoice lines?
  • What happens to the record when the crew resubmits?
  • What do you get, and on what terms, in writing before you commit?

Ask the vendor to show you one of your own jobs going from the map to the invoice. That covers the whole list in one conversation.
