BEAD Gets a Second Round: What It Means for Your Pipeline

By Gil Ramirez September 25, 2026 5 min read

As of September 3, 2026, the Trump administration told states they can use remaining BEAD funds on a new round of deployment projects. If you're a telecom contractor with crews ready to work, that's a new window opening. The question is whether you'll be positioned when it does.

What's Happening and Why It Affects Your Crew Slate

BEAD, the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment program, is a $42 billion federal program aimed at connecting unserved rural locations. States spent $18.2 billion on their first round of approvals. That left a significant pool sitting without a deployment path.

The administration's September 3 announcement gives states a lane to route some of those remaining funds into a new bidding round. NTIA, the National Telecommunications and Information Administration, said it would outline other allowable uses in a future update. That update hasn't dropped yet.

So here's the call you have to make: do you wait for full guidance, or do you start positioning now with the ISPs likely to bid this new round?

The Pipeline Math You Should Be Running Right Now

Colorado just cancelled a $103 million BEAD award after its broadband office found problems including missing funds. That award went to Maverix Broadband and got referred to the state's Bureau of Investigation and the U.S. Treasury Inspectors General. That's one more project that needs to be reawarded or absorbed by another provider.

Cancelled awards don't disappear. They get rebid. And when they do, the ISP that wins needs boots on the ground fast.

If you're running 10 to 25 crews in a state with remaining BEAD allocation, your ISP partners are about to hear from NTIA. When that call comes, they'll reach for contractors they already trust. Not ones they're meeting for the first time at a pre-bid meeting.

What You Don't Know Yet (and What to Do About It)

The NTIA guidance on other allowable uses hasn't been published. State broadband offices are waiting on it too. So no one has a full picture of how much money flows into deployment versus other uses.

What you do know: a new bidding round is confirmed. Some states have more remaining BEAD funds than others. Alaska is one of the states tagged in this announcement. If you work there or adjacent markets, that's worth a conversation with whoever runs your state broadband office relationship.

You also know the states that have approved initial BEAD plans are already spending $18.2 billion. That first wave of construction is underway. A second wave means extended runway, not a restart.

Three Things to Do Before the Guidance Drops

  • Map which of your current ISP partners have remaining BEAD allocation in their state. Ask them directly. The answer tells you who's about to get busy.
  • Pull your AR aging report and look at your working capital position. A new round means new mobilization costs before first invoice. If your DSO is running 55 days or higher, shore that up before you take on more work.
  • Document your crew capacity and your completed BEAD-adjacent work. When ISPs start bidding, they need a one-page summary of what you've built, where, and how fast. Have that ready.

The Bigger Picture for Your Business

I've seen contractors sit out the first BEAD round because the paperwork felt heavy and the timelines felt uncertain. Some of them are watching competitors add two and three crews right now.

A second bidding round is a second chance. The ISPs who move fast on this round will pick their contractors early. Be one of those contractors, not a late call when they're already behind schedule.
