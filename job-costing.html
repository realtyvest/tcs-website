What TCS Gives Job Costing Today

Job costing exposes the gap between what you bill and what the job actually costs. The billed side has to be right first, and it has to come from the work, not from a count typed in later.

Billed Revenue

This is what the ISP pays you. It comes from the rate card and the codes and quantities the crew marked, so each invoice line traces back to a mark on the map.

Production Data by Rate Code

Job costing is only as good as the production numbers under it. Footage and units are tallied by rate code from the crew's marks, on the same job record the invoice comes from.

One Record per Job

The office job list shows, for each job, how many times its map has been submitted and the date of the last submission.

How the Production Side Works

The production side of job costing comes straight from the field.

Mark the Map

On site, the crew places each code and quantity on the map where the work was done.

Submit From the Field

At job end, the crew submits the marked map once. Each submission is saved with who submitted it and when.

Tally by Rate Code

Footage and units add up by rate code from the marks. A correction is a resubmit, and the earlier version stays on the record as superseded.

Invoice From the Tally

The office drafts the invoice from the submitted tally, each line priced from the rate card for that ISP or prime. The invoice can go out the day the job is submitted.

Why This Matters

Unprofitable work often shows up only after the job is done, when it's too late to fix. The first step is knowing exactly what was built and billed on each job, by rate code. That is the part TCS handles today. The Operations Audit looks at where the cost side of your jobs is tracked now.
