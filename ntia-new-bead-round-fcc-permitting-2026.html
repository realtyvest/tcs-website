NTIA's New BEAD Round and FCC Permitting: What It Means for Your Crews

By Gil Ramirez September 23, 2026 5 min read

The NTIA just opened a new round of BEAD funding to cover locations that were missed in the first pass. At the same time, the FCC is moving toward NEPA permitting reforms that could shorten build timelines. Both moves land on your desk this week.

A Second BEAD Round Means More Work Coming, Not Later

BEAD (Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment) is the $42.5 billion federal program pushing broadband to unserved and underserved locations. The first round didn't reach every qualifying address. The NTIA has now opened a new funding round targeted at those newly identified unserved locations.

That's not a technicality. It's a new wave of construction contracts that didn't exist a month ago.

Here's the call you have to make: your ISP customers are going to receive new subgrantee obligations tied to these locations. They'll need crews to build them. If you're not already in conversations with your top two or three ISP customers about their BEAD buildout timelines, start this week. The contractor who gets the call first usually gets the work.

Don't wait for an RFQ to land in your inbox. That's too late. Ask your ISP contact directly: are you receiving new BEAD allocations under the second round, and when do you need boots on the ground?

FCC Permitting Reform Could Cut Your Build Timeline

The FCC is moving toward a vote on NEPA permitting reforms for broadband builds. NEPA (National Environmental Policy Act) reviews are one of the biggest non-labor delays in fiber construction. Aerial and underground crews both hit these walls on rural builds.

One aerial crew I work with in the Midwest sat on a four-mile segment for 11 weeks waiting on a NEPA clearance. The materials were staged. The crew was ready. The permit wasn't.

If these reforms pass, shorter review windows change your scheduling math. You can commit to tighter timelines with ISP customers. You can reduce the gap between mobilization and revenue. That gap is where working capital gets eaten.

Watch the FCC vote date. When it passes, update your project kickoff assumptions and tell your ISP customers you can move faster than last year's schedule assumed.

Clearwave and Visionary Combining Means a Bigger Customer Is Forming

Clearwave Fiber and Visionary Broadband are merging to serve 700,000 addresses across 17 states. That's a large footprint for one entity to manage with its own workforce.

Mergers like this almost always create contract gaps. Internal crews get realigned. Project managers change. Vendors get re-evaluated. That window, usually six to twelve months post-close, is when outside telecom contractors win new work from operators who didn't previously use them.

If you're operating in any of those 17 states and you don't have a relationship with Clearwave or Visionary today, now is the time to get in front of whoever is handling construction post-merger. A combined entity at that scale will need reliable crew capacity.

Rural California and New Mexico Show State Money Is Still Moving

A public-private partnership is advancing a rural California fiber network. New Mexico awarded nearly $6 million in grants for public Wi-Fi projects. State-level programs are still writing checks while federal timelines stretch.

If you're chasing only federal BEAD dollars, you're leaving state-funded work on the table. Check your state broadband office's current open solicitations. Most states have dedicated broadband offices now, and several have grant cycles that close before the end of Q4 2026.

The Position to Be In Right Now

A second BEAD round, permitting reform, and a major merger happening in the same month isn't coincidence. The construction pipeline for 2027 is being shaped right now by which telecom contractors are in the room with their ISP customers and which ones are waiting for the phone to ring.

Get your capacity conversation on the calendar before October. Know your available crew-weeks for Q1 and Q2 2027. Walk into that ISP meeting with numbers, not availability.
