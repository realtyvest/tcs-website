Arkansas Has $650M in BEAD Funds and a Plan. Here's What It Means for You.

By Gil Ramirez August 16, 2026 5 min read

Arkansas is sitting on $650 million in unspent BEAD funds and says it has a plan for where that money goes next. If you are running crews anywhere near that state, this is the question on your desk this week: are you positioned to capture work when those dollars start moving?

What BEAD Is and Why $650 Million Still Sitting Matters

BEAD stands for Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment. It is the federal program funneling roughly $42 billion to states to build out broadband infrastructure to unserved and underserved locations. Each state got an allocation. States then run their own procurement to get that money out the door through ISPs, who in turn hire telecom contractors to do the physical build.

Arkansas received its allocation. According to reporting from Broadband Breakfast, the state has $650 million in funds not yet committed to projects. That is not a bad sign by itself. Many states are still working through their subgrantee selection process. But it does mean a large wave of work has not yet been assigned, and Arkansas is now publicly outlining how it plans to deploy those dollars.

The full details of Arkansas's specific proposal were not available in the source at time of writing. What is confirmed: $650 million is in play, and the state is actively planning its next move.

Why This Matters for Your Pipeline

Here is how BEAD money reaches your crews. The state awards grants to ISPs, called subgrantees. Those ISPs contract out the physical construction to telecom contractors. That means every dollar Arkansas commits creates a downstream demand for boots on the ground: fiber splicers, directional bore operators, aerial strand crews, trench teams.

$650 million in Arkansas alone is not a small number. For context, a typical rural fiber project runs $1,000 to $3,000 per passing depending on terrain and density. At $1,500 per passing, $650 million funds roughly 430,000 passings. That is a lot of drops, splice enclosures, and bore footage.

The contractors who will capture that work are not the ones who show up when RFPs drop. They are the ones already in conversation with ISPs who are waiting on their Arkansas subgrantee awards. If you are not in those conversations now, you are starting late.

What You Should Be Doing Before the Work Goes Out

Three things worth doing before Arkansas awards firm up.

  • Map your regional ISP relationships. Which ISPs operate in Arkansas? Which have already applied or been selected as potential subgrantees? Those are the calls to make this month, not after award letters go out.
  • Check your bonding and insurance capacity. BEAD-funded work comes with federal compliance requirements. Subgrantees pass those requirements downstream. If your COI limits or bonding capacity are not sized for a $3 million to $8 million project, that is a back office fix to start now.
  • Get your WIP schedule clean. ISPs awarding BEAD subcontracts want to see that you can run multiple concurrent jobs without cash flow problems. A clean work-in-progress schedule and a tight AR aging report are the documents that close that conversation.

The Honest Caveat

The source article confirms the $650 million figure and that Arkansas has a plan. The specific uses, timeline, and procurement structure were not detailed in the available reporting. Watch for Arkansas's official BEAD implementation plan updates directly from the Arkansas Rural Connect program or the NTIA, which is the National Telecommunications and Information Administration, the federal agency overseeing BEAD statewide rollouts.

Delays are real across every state. That does not mean you wait. It means you use the delay to get positioned so you are not scrambling when the window opens.

The contractors who win BEAD work are already in the room before the award letters go out.
