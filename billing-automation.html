Contractor Billing, Not Subscriber Billing

For contractors doing aerial, underground, and drop bury work for ISPs and primes. It does not bill an ISP's subscribers.

How a Fiber Invoice Is Built: Code, Quantity, Contract Rate

Fiber work is billed in units your contract lists: feet of cable and conduit, and each for drops, splices, and locates.

Quantities come from the marks the crew made on the map and submitted once from the field. Tally = marks. Nobody retypes a sheet on Friday. The crew marks the map in TCS Map Annotator. For the field side, see map to invoice.

Each invoice line is the code, times the quantity, at the contract rate for that code.

The marked-up map exports as a PDF you can send with the invoice. For the rest of what the ISP asks for, see the package checklist.

The Billing Workflow

Six steps, from the marked map to the invoice.

Mark the Map

The crew places each code and quantity where the work was done.

Submit

Once, from the field. The tally is re-counted from the marks, so tally = marks.

Review

The office drafts the invoice from that tally and checks it before issuing.

Price

Each line is priced from the rate card for that ISP or prime.

Invoice

One line per code, one invoice per submitted job. The invoice can go out the day the job is submitted. Once issued, it is locked.

Send

The invoice package is ready for you to submit wherever the ISP or prime requires.

What Gets Checked Before It Goes Out

Lines come from the marks, so the invoice ties to the marked map by design.

Every Mark Carries a Code

Every mark has to carry a code from your rate card. A mark without one can't be submitted.

Unknown Codes Are Refused

Codes that aren't on your rate cards are refused at Submit.

The Tally Is Re-Counted

The tally is re-counted from the marks on the server. The count on the iPad is a preview.

Only the Latest Submission Bills

If the crew resubmits the map, the new submission replaces the old tally. Only the latest one bills.

One Live Invoice per Job

One live invoice per job, so the same job can't be billed twice.

Issued Invoices Are Locked

Issued invoices are locked. A later rate change doesn't rewrite them.

Rate Cards and POs

Set up a rate card for each ISP or prime you work for. Every line is a code from your contract rate card, times the quantity, at the rate for that code.

Change a rate once and every new invoice uses it. Issued invoices keep the rate they were issued at.

Each job carries the PO number it bills against.

If You Bill Subs, or Bill as a Sub

If you work as a sub, the same marks become your invoice to the prime. If you run subs, see sub invoicing.

Questions Contractors Ask

Is this ISP billing software?

No. ISP billing software bills an internet provider's subscribers every month. This page is about the other side of the work: the contractor who builds the network and invoices the ISP or prime for it, by rate code and quantity. TCS is built for that contractor. It does not bill subscribers, run autopay, or manage subscriber accounts.

Why can't I invoice fiber footage until the as-builts are approved?

Your ISP or prime may want proof of what was built before it pays, so the invoice has to line up with the as-built or the package it requires. When the invoice quantities come from the same marked map the crew submitted, the proof and the invoice say the same thing. Your contract sets exactly what has to be approved first.

How do fiber contractors bill the ISP?

By unit. The contract or rate card lists a code for each kind of work, such as feet of cable or conduit, or each drop, splice, or locate, with a rate for each code. Each invoice line is the quantity of a code times its contract rate, usually tied to a PO or work order. The quantity is where invoices go wrong, so capture it where the work happens.

How does unit-price billing work after the work is done?

Under unit-price billing, you and the owner agree on a rate for each unit of work, and you bill for the units actually completed. After the work is done, you count or measure what was built, multiply each quantity by its agreed rate, and submit the invoice with whatever proof the contract requires. If the final quantity differs from the estimate, the contract says how that difference is handled.

What is a unit price contract?

A unit price contract sets a fixed rate for each unit of work, such as a foot of cable or a splice, instead of one price for the whole job. The total isn't known up front. It depends on the quantities actually completed and accepted. It suits work where the exact scope is hard to pin down before the crew starts. The terms of your own contract control.

What's the difference between a price variance and a quantity variance on an invoice, and how is each resolved?

A price variance means the rate on an invoice line doesn't match the agreed rate for that item. A quantity variance means the billed quantity doesn't match what was authorized, ordered, or accepted. A price variance is usually settled by correcting the rate or pointing to the contract or change order that set it. A quantity variance is usually settled with proof of what was built or an approved change. Your contract sets the process.

What does the ISP need to see before it pays?

It depends on the ISP or prime and the contract. Common items are the marked-up map or as-built and quantities by code. Check your contract for the exact list, and use the package checklist. With TCS, the marked-up map the crew submitted exports as a PDF to send with the invoice.

How do subs invoice a prime?

The same way a prime invoices the ISP: by code and quantity, at the rate in the sub's agreement with the prime, with the proof the prime asks for. The prime's terms set how often and where the sub submits. With TCS, a sub's crew marks the map and submits once, and those marks become the sub's invoice to the prime.
