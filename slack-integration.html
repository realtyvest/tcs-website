Slack Integration: Status the ISP Can Read Without Calling Anyone

The ISP's project manager asks for an update on segment seven for the third time this week. The answer already exists on the job record. This module posts it where they can read it.

The Status Call Chain

The ISP asks the prime's PM. The prime's PM asks the sub. The sub asks the crew lead. The crew lead answers from the truck. The answer travels back up the chain, gets reworded twice, and lands in an email the ISP reads the next morning. Multiply that by every open job and it is a real share of the PM's week.

None of those people needed to be asked. The crew lead already submitted the job. The record already knows the photos are in and the map is marked. The only missing piece is that nobody outside the record could see it.

What the Integration Does

  1. A gate closes on the recordThe crew submits. The map is marked. The invoice goes out. Each of those is a state change on the job, with a timestamp and an owner.
  2. The right channel gets the postAn internal channel gets the nudge for the next owner: "Job 4821 is waiting on the as-built. Owner: Marcus." A per-customer channel gets the status the ISP wants: "Segment 7, 12 drops complete, closeout submitted."
  3. The ISP reads, and does not callThe ISP's PM checks the channel on their own time. If they have a question, they ask it there, against the job, and the answer comes from the record.

What gets posted is decided once, per customer. Some ISPs want every gate. Some want a daily roll-up. Some want only exceptions. The channel is shaped to the customer, and the crew never has to think about it.

Why It Is Slack and Not Another Dashboard

Because the ISP's PM is already in Slack, and so is your office. A dashboard nobody opens is a report. A message in the channel someone already reads is a status update. The record stays the source of truth; Slack is just where the state change shows up.

The same mechanism drives the internal nudges. A job stuck at photos pings the crew lead who owns that gate, not the whole company. A job ready to bill pings accounting. The PM sees it all on the board and in the channel, and stops being the person who relays.

What Has to Be True for It to Work

  1. The job record has gates with owners. Slack posts state changes. If the state is not on the record, there is nothing to post. See job tracking.
  2. Each customer has a channel and a rule. What they see, and how often, agreed once.
  3. The post links back to the record. The message is a summary. The job card is the answer.

Questions contractors ask

Does the ISP need access to our system?
No. They see a Slack channel with the status posts you have agreed to share. If they want more, the post links to a read-only view of the job. Nothing internal, no pricing, no sub information, leaves the record.
What if the ISP does not use Slack?
The same state changes can go out as an email digest or to whatever the ISP already reads. Slack is the common case because most ISP project teams and most contractor offices are already in it.
Will the crew get buried in notifications?
Crews get one kind of message: a job on their list is waiting on them. Everything else goes to the office or the customer channel. The rule is that a notification names one owner and one next step.
