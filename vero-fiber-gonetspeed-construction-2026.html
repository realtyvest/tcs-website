Vero Fiber and GoNetspeed Are Building. Are You On Their Bid List?

By Gil Ramirez August 25, 2026 5 min read

Two FTTH builds just went active. Vero Fiber kicked off Phase 2 of their Eureka fiber-to-the-home project this month. GoNetspeed broke ground in Parlin, New Jersey. Both moves signal contractor work on the table right now, not next quarter.

What These Builds Mean for Your Pipeline

When an ISP starts a named phase of a build, crews are already on the ground or weeks away from being on the ground. If you're not already in conversation with their construction team, you're behind. That's the honest read.

Vero Fiber's Phase 2 in Eureka tells you one thing clearly: they closed out Phase 1 well enough to fund the next push. ISPs that move from phase to phase without a long pause are the ones worth chasing. They have capital, they have a plan, and they need crews who can execute.

GoNetspeed starting construction in Parlin, New Jersey is a separate opportunity. New Jersey has dense housing, permitting that can drag, and a shortage of aerial and underground crews who know how to work tight suburban routes. If you run crews in the mid-Atlantic region, this build is worth a direct call to their construction management team this week.

How to Get In Front of Active Builders

Here's the call you have to make. Do you wait for a bid request to land in your inbox, or do you go find the construction manager and introduce your crew capacity before they've finalized their contractor list?

Every telecom contractor we work with who wins new ISP relationships does the same thing. They call the network construction or outside plant team directly. They lead with capacity, geography, and one or two relevant projects they've completed in similar terrain. They don't pitch. They make it easy for the ISP to say yes.

A specific move: look up Vero Fiber's and GoNetspeed's LinkedIn pages. Find the director of network construction or VP of outside plant. Send a short message. Name your crew count, your work type (aerial, underground, drop installation), and your service territory. Ask if they have a preferred contractor list and how to get on it. That's it. Three sentences.

The Bigger Pattern to Watch

August 2026 FTTH construction activity is running across multiple ISPs at once. That's not a coincidence. A lot of the capital that was locked up in BEAD (the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment program, the $42.5 billion federal broadband fund) delays has pushed ISPs to move faster on their privately funded builds while they wait for state programs to settle.

That creates a window. Private ISP builds don't carry BEAD compliance overhead. No prevailing wage calculations, no Buy America documentation, no quarterly reporting to a state broadband office. You quote the work, you build the work, you invoice the work. Cash moves faster.

I've seen contractors pick up six months of crew utilization by focusing on private ISP builds during BEAD's slow periods. Your DSO on private ISP work is typically shorter than on publicly funded projects too, which matters when you're managing working capital across 10 or more crews.

What to Do Before Monday

Pull up Vero Fiber and GoNetspeed. Find out who runs their outside plant construction. Make contact this week while the builds are in early stages. Early-stage is when contractor slots are still open.

If you already sub to a general contractor on ISP work and you're not getting direct ISP relationships, this is the right moment to ask why. Direct relationships mean better margins and faster payment terms.
