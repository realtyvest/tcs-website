Astound Dropped $165M in Texas BEAD. What That Means for Your Crew.

By Gil Ramirez August 16, 2026 5 min read

Astound turned down $165 million in Texas BEAD awards. Resound dropped out too. When ISPs walk away from awarded funding, the work does not disappear. It gets reassigned. That reassignment is the moment every telecom contractor in Texas needs to watch.

What Happened and Why It Matters to Your Pipeline

BEAD stands for Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment. It is the $42.5 billion federal program sending money through state broadband offices to ISPs, who then hire telecom contractors to build the network. Texas has one of the largest BEAD allocations in the country.

Astound has not explained the refusal in detail. Broadband Breakfast reported the $165 million figure and that Resound also exited. The reasons are not public, so treat any explanation you hear as rumor until an award reassignment shows up.

What is confirmed: two providers walked away from awarded Texas BEAD money. That is not a footnote. That is a pipeline event.

Reassigned Awards Mean New Subgrantee Relationships

When a provider drops a BEAD award, the state broadband office, in Texas that is the Comptroller's Broadband Development Office, does not simply pocket the money. The office goes back through its process. It either re-awards to a backup applicant or rebids the locations.

A subgrantee is the ISP that receives the state grant and becomes the entity responsible for building and operating the network. New subgrantees mean new contractor relationships. If you are not in front of the incoming ISP before they win the award, you are starting late.

Here is the call you have to make right now: do you know which providers are positioned to absorb the Astound and Resound locations? If not, that is your first task this week.

What Pullbacks Signal About ISP Risk Appetite

I have seen this play out in other states. When a mid-size ISP refuses or returns an award, it usually comes down to one of three pressures: construction cost overruns against a fixed grant amount, trouble lining up contractor capacity, or a business model that does not survive the BEAD build timeline.

That last one matters to contractors. If an ISP is shaky on capital or crew planning, and they win the reassigned award, you are walking into a slow-pay situation. Check AR aging before you commit crews. A 90-day DSO on a BEAD build will drain your working capital fast.

The Texas Comptroller's office has not announced a public timeline for reassignment as of August 13, 2026, based on available source material. Watch for a formal notice of award reallocation. That is your trigger.

Position Before the Reassignment Lands, Not After

Telecom contractors who win BEAD work are not the ones who respond to RFPs fastest. They are the ones already known to the ISP before the RFP publishes. That means conversations now, while the award pool is in flux.

Pull a list of regional ISPs operating in the Astound and Resound service footprints in Texas. Call their construction or operations contacts this week. Ask what their capacity plans look like for Q4 2026 and into 2027. Do not pitch. Ask questions. That is how you get into the room before the contract is written.

The funding did not vanish. It is moving. The contractors who move with it will capture the work. The ones who wait for a signed contract to appear will be filling in gaps at someone else's rate.

Every BEAD pullback is a reassignment waiting to happen. Move before the paperwork does.
