Vero Broadband Buys MontanaSky: What It Means for Montana Contractors

By Gil Ramirez August 26, 2026 5 min read

Vero Broadband (doing business as Vero Fiber) and MontanaSky have signed a definitive merger agreement. If you're running crews in northwest Montana or bidding work with either company, this changes your landscape.

What's Happening and Why It Matters to Your Pipeline

The two companies announced a signed agreement to merge. The deal is executed. It closes after standard regulatory approvals go through. No closing date has been named publicly.

Vero Fiber operates fixed fiber networks. MontanaSky serves rural northwest Montana with wireless and fiber-based broadband. Combined, they cover a meaningful stretch of underserved territory in a state that still has a large BEAD allocation sitting in play. BEAD, the Broadband Equity, Access, and Deployment program, is the $42.5 billion federal fund flowing through state broadband offices to build last-mile infrastructure in areas like this.

When two regional ISPs merge, the field work doesn't stop. It consolidates. One network plan, one construction budget, one vendor relationship.

The Call You Have to Make Right Now

If you're already working with MontanaSky or Vero Fiber, here's the question on your desk this week: who on the new combined team controls the construction vendor list?

Mergers shuffle that list. The contractor who stays close to the project manager through the transition keeps the work. The one who waits to see what shakes out often finds out too late that the new operations lead brought in their own crews.

Get in front of someone at both companies now, before regulatory close. Find out who is leading network buildout for the combined entity. Get a name. Put a meeting on the calendar.

Montana's BEAD Timeline Adds Urgency

Montana's BEAD program, run through the Montana Broadband Program office, is in active development. A merger between two of the state's regional ISPs right now could affect who submits subgrantee applications, what service areas get prioritized, and what construction capacity those companies need to line up.

Subgrantee means the ISP that receives state BEAD funds and then hires contractors to build the network. If the combined Vero-MontanaSky entity grows its footprint and BEAD award potential, that's more fiber miles, more drop installs, more splicing work coming down the line in northwest Montana.

That's not guaranteed. But it's the scenario worth positioning for now, not after close.

What a Consolidating ISP Landscape Means for Contractor Leverage

More mergers are coming across the country. Smaller ISPs are getting absorbed into larger ones. Each merger is a short window where your relationship is either locked in or at risk.

I've seen this play out in other markets. The contractor who had five years with the acquired company assumed the relationship carried over. It didn't. The new parent company had preferred vendors. The incumbent contractor had to re-qualify, rebid, and in two cases I know of, lost the work entirely.

Don't assume continuity. Confirm it.

  • Identify your primary contact at both Vero Fiber and MontanaSky.
  • Ask directly who owns vendor decisions post-close.
  • Get your COI, safety records, and past performance docs updated and ready to send.
  • If you don't have a relationship with either company yet, the pre-close window is your opening.

Keep Watching the Close

Regulatory approvals on deals like this typically run 60 to 180 days depending on what filings are required. Stay on the timeline. When close happens, construction planning accelerates fast.
