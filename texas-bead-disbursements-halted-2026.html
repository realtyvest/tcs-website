Texas Froze BEAD Funds. Here Is What You Do Next.

By Gil Ramirez August 14, 2026 5 min read

Texas stopped moving BEAD money. If you are a telecom contractor in Texas counting on that pipeline, the freeze is not a rumor. It is the operating reality you are scheduling around right now.

Broadband Breakfast reported the halt and a restructuring of the Texas broadband office under a new Comptroller. Here is what we know for certain: disbursements are paused, the office is being recast, and no telecom contractor in Texas has a clear timeline for when funds move again.

That is enough to act on.

What a Comptroller Restructuring Actually Means for Your Backlog

BEAD works like this: the federal government (NTIA, the National Telecommunications and Information Administration) sends money to each state. The state broadband office then manages awards to ISPs and other subgrantees. Subgrantees hire telecom contractors to do the build.

When the state office restructures, the chain stops. Not because the federal money disappeared. Because the state-level staff, processes, and authority are in flux. New leadership means new sign-off chains. New sign-off chains mean delayed purchase orders, delayed notices to proceed, and delayed payment draws.

Every contractor waiting on a Texas BEAD-linked project is now sitting on WIP (work in progress) with no clear payment trigger in sight.

The Call You Have to Make This Week

Here is the question on your desk: how many weeks of payroll can you float on your current line of credit if Texas BEAD jobs go quiet for 90 days?

Pull your AR aging report today. Look at every open invoice tied to a Texas broadband project. Flag which ones trace back to BEAD funding. If you do not know which projects are BEAD-adjacent, call your ISP contacts and ask directly. Do not assume.

Then run a simple scenario. If those invoices sit 60 more days, what does your working capital position look like? If the answer makes you uncomfortable, you have two moves.

First, accelerate collections on every non-BEAD invoice now. Do not wait for the Wednesday AR meeting. Make the calls Monday morning. Second, talk to your lender this week, not after the crunch hits. A proactive conversation about your line of credit is a different meeting than a desperate one.

Your Pipeline Outside Texas Still Moves

Thirty-six other states are at varying stages of BEAD deployment. Some are further along than Texas was before this halt. Louisiana, Virginia, and Nevada have been moving faster through their initial award phases. If Texas BEAD work was a significant share of your forward pipeline, now is the time to ask which neighboring states have active solicitations.

One underground bore contractor we work with kept two states in active pursuit at all times, exactly for situations like this. When one state stalled, the second pipeline absorbed the capacity. It is not glamorous contingency planning. It is just how you stay off the ropes when one program hits a wall.

Also worth noting: E-Rate, emergency connectivity, and CAF II closeout work are not BEAD. If your ISP relationships run across program types, now is the time to ask what else is moving.

Watch the Texas Broadband Office Closely

When a new Comptroller recasts an office, the org chart changes but the federal deadlines do not. NTIA has its own milestones baked into state agreements. Texas will feel pressure to restart disbursements. The freeze is likely a pause, not a cancellation.

But likely is not a schedule. Track the Texas Comptroller's office announcements directly. When the new leadership publishes a resumption timeline, you want to be the first contractor whose ISP partner hears that you are ready to mobilize.

That positioning is built now, during the freeze, not after the thaw.
