Why a Full Fiber Schedule Can Empty Your Bank Account Before You Bill

ByGil Ramirez
PublishedOctober 15, 2026
Read Time6 minutes

The short answer: before a fiber sub can invoice, money goes out while crews wait on make-ready, for the paperwork funded projects require, and for work that closeout will hold. Payroll and fuel do not wait for billing. The fix is not more work. It is fewer days between spending and billing.

This page is general information, not financial or legal advice. Program and state rules vary.

Cost One: Make-Ready and Pole Work

Before fiber goes on a pole, the pole often needs make-ready: surveys, moving existing lines, sometimes a new pole. The company asking to attach usually requests it, then waits on surveys and the pole owner. If your crews are mobilized and the poles are not ready, they sit idle, and you still pay them.

Under the FCC pole attachment rules (47 CFR 1.1411(e)), the attacher pays the pole owner's make-ready estimate before the work starts, and the rules set a timeline for the work. FCC rules cover many poles, but states that regulate pole attachments themselves, co-ops and city-owned utilities can set different terms (47 U.S.C. 224(a)(1) and (c)).

Cost Two: Compliance Paperwork on Funded Jobs

On BEAD and other federally funded jobs, the prime or subgrantee asks subs for paperwork: insurance certificates (COIs), W9s, licenses or certs, and labor records. Federal award terms flow down through your contract under the Uniform Guidance (2 CFR 200) and NTIA BEAD guidance.

  • Insurance certificates and W9s.
  • Licenses or certs.
  • Labor records.
  • Buy America documentation, where your contract or grant requires it.

Federal grant terms flow down through your contract, so what you are asked for varies by state program and contract. Depending on your contract, work without accepted paperwork may not be billable yet. The office hours spent chasing it are a real cost too. The BEAD subcontractor guide covers how BEAD work reaches a sub.

Cost Three: Closeout Holds

By the time the work is done, you have usually paid the crews already. Some of that money is held until the closeout package is accepted, so it is money you already spent. A late or incomplete package keeps it out of your account longer. The fiber closeout package checklist covers what the ISP expects, and what happens after you invoice covers terms and retainage release.

What You Can Control

  • Start paperwork before you mobilize, and get it accepted before the job starts.
  • Track make-ready status for each pole line.
  • Build the closeout package as you go, not at the end.
  • Bill the day work is accepted.

Questions Contractors Ask

Why am I out of cash when my schedule is full?

Many of your costs come before you can bill. Crews, fuel, make-ready waits, and paperwork all cost money first. The invoice comes later, and the payment later still.

Is make-ready the sub's cost?

It depends on the contract and on who owns the poles. Under FCC pole attachment rules, the company asking to attach pays the pole owner's make-ready estimate before the work starts, and the prime may or may not pass that cost through. Some states, co-ops and city-owned utilities set their own rules. Read your contract before the job starts, and track idle crew days caused by make-ready.

What paperwork do BEAD-funded jobs ask subs for?

Primes and subgrantees often ask for insurance certificates, tax forms, licenses or certs, and labor records. Exact lists vary by state program and contract. Depending on your contract, missing paperwork can hold up billing.

How do closeout holds hurt cash flow?

You pay crews when the work is done, but some money is held until the closeout package is accepted. A late or incomplete package keeps that money out of your account longer.

What can I control before the first invoice?

Get paperwork accepted before you mobilize, build closeout docs as you work, and bill the day work is accepted. Those steps shorten the gap between spending and billing.
