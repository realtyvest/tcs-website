How to Track Telecom RFPs and Bids

RFPs and bid requests arrive by email, portal, and phone call. Miss a due date and the work goes to somebody else. Win the work and lose track of what was authorized, and the invoice gets questioned. A simple tracking habit covers both.

Where Telecom RFPs Come From

Work requests reach a telecom contractor from a lot of places: ISP and prime portals, emailed bid packages, repeat customers' work orders, municipal and utility bid boards, and BEAD subgrantees hiring for their builds. The BEAD subcontractor guide covers how that last group picks its subs.

Many contractors watch more than one of these at once. That is why a single log matters more than any one source.

What to Log for Every RFP

Who and where

The customer, the project, the location, and where the request came from: the portal, the contact, or the email thread.

Dates

Questions due, bid due, expected award, and expected start. The bid due date is the one that loses work when it is missed.

Scope

The rate codes and quantities in the RFP's own units: feet for cable and conduit, each for drops, splices, and locates.

Status and decision

Reviewing, bidding, submitted, won, lost, or no bid, with the reason for the decision.

Deciding Whether to Bid

Before you price it, check that you have crews free in the window, that the codes fit your rate card, and that you can meet the insurance limits and documents the customer asks for. Paperwork standards count too: photos, test results, and as-builts all take crew time, so price them into the unit rates. Customer documents are covered in contractor compliance tracking.

After the Award: Authorized Quantities

The awarded RFP or PO sets what you are authorized to bill. Keep the authorized codes and quantities where the office can see them for the life of the job.

Compare the tally to the authorized quantities before you issue the invoice. If the crew did more than was authorized, get the change order approved before you bill it, not after.

What Has to Be True for It to Work

Use the RFP's codes

If your rate card uses different names, map them once when you log the award, not on every invoice.

Capture quantities in the RFP's units

Feet for cable and conduit, each for drops, splices, and locates.

Change orders update what is authorized

An approved change order adds to the authorized quantities. Keep it with the job, not in an email thread.

One person owns the log

The log is only as current as the person who updates it. Update it the day an RFP arrives.

Questions contractors ask

What is the simplest way to start tracking RFPs?

One shared log with a row per RFP: customer, project, due date, scope, status, and the decision. A spreadsheet works if one person owns it and it is updated the day an RFP arrives.

What happens when the crew does more than the RFP authorized?

Compare the tally to the authorized quantities before you issue the invoice. If the work went over, get a change order approved first, or bill to the authorized quantity and note the difference.

Should we track the bids we lose?

Yes. Log the result and the reason if the customer gives one. Over time the log shows which customers, regions, and job types you win, and where your bids miss.
